Privacy policy

Your music stays on your PC.

This covers the Mooxr desktop app, the Mooxr Chrome extension and this website. Last updated 6 November 2026.

The short version

  • Mooxr doesn’t collect, sell or share your personal data.
  • No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking.
  • Converting happens on your own PC. Nothing is sent to us.
  • Mooxr only goes online when you ask it to, such as fetching a link you pasted.

The desktop app

What stays on your PC

  • Your settings: theme, light or dark, save folder and where tracks are sent. Windows keeps them for your user account under HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\Mooxr.
  • The MP3s you make, saved in the folder you choose. Each file is tagged with its title, artist and cover art, plus the link or file name it came from, so Mooxr can tell you if you’ve already mooxed something.
  • FFmpeg, the converting tool, if you let Mooxr fetch it. It goes in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Mooxr\ffmpeg.

When it goes online

Only when you do something that needs it:

  • Get FFmpeg downloads FFmpeg from gyan.dev and checks it against the published checksum.
  • Pasting a link makes Mooxr contact that site directly, using yt-dlp, to read the title, artist and thumbnail and to download the audio.
  • Sending to YouTube Music, Deezer or SoundCloud opens that service’s upload page in your browser. You choose whether to upload.
  • Clicking the logo opens this website in your browser.

Like any website visit, those sites see your IP address and handle it under their own privacy policies. Nothing about what you convert is sent to Mooxr’s developer.

The Chrome extension

The extension hands links from Chrome to the Mooxr app on the same PC. It doesn’t send anything to us or to any other server.

What it uses and why

nativeMessaging

Talks to the Mooxr app through a small helper installed on your PC. The link, title, artist and any cover picture you choose go only to the app.

activeTab scripting

Reads the address and title of the tab you’re on when you open the extension or use its menu, so it knows what to moox.

contextMenus

Adds “Moox this link”, “Moox this page” and “Moox this video” to the right-click menu.

storage

Keeps your queue, settings and a cover picture you picked (until the app takes it) in Chrome on this device. Nothing is synced to your Google account.

notifications

Tells you when a track is finished or something went wrong.

Site access

On YouTube, YouTube Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp and Mixcloud, the extension looks at the page to work out whether it’s a song, and puts a dot on its toolbar button if so. On YouTube it may load that video’s page from YouTube to read its details, as your browser would. It only runs on other sites if you turn that on in Settings → Music, and you can turn it off again there.

The helper writes error details to %LOCALAPPDATA%\Mooxr\chrome-host.log on your PC, to help fix problems. That file never leaves your PC unless you send it to someone yourself.

Mooxr’s use of data follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Data isn’t sold, used for ads or credit checks, or shared with anyone.

This website

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses for security, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

The site has no cookies, analytics or third-party scripts. If you switch between light and dark, your choice is saved in your browser’s local storage so the page looks the same next time. Clear your site data to remove it.

Removing your data

Everything Mooxr keeps is on your PC, so you’re in control of it:

  • Removing the extension from Chrome deletes everything it stored.
  • Deleting %LOCALAPPDATA%\Mooxr removes FFmpeg and the helper’s log.
  • Deleting the registry key HKEY_CURRENT_USER\Software\Mooxr resets the app’s settings.
  • Your MP3s are yours. Delete or keep them as you like.

Children

Mooxr isn’t aimed at children under 13, and it doesn’t collect personal data from anyone.
